# Literature evidence extraction

## Define the question first

Use one primary evidence question per search: allele identity, functional effect,
de novo occurrence, segregation, phase/in-trans evidence, phenotype specificity,
case enrichment, or benign evidence.

## Claim record

For every potentially usable claim, capture:

- PMID/DOI and article type;
- exact allele and representation used by the paper;
- transcript/build when reported;
- disease and inheritance context;
- proband/family/cohort count;
- parentage confirmation and phase method when relevant;
- phenotype and ascertainment;
- experimental model, assay, controls, replicates, and result for functional work;
- author conclusion separately from observed result;
- limitations and applicability to the current criterion;
- article section, table, figure, or supplement location.

## Evidence distinctions

- A review can identify primary sources but is not a substitute for their case
  details.
- A database citation does not prove that the cited article contains the needed
  evidence.
- A functional effect is not automatically disease-mechanism evidence.
- Several patients in one family are not independent occurrences.
- “De novo” without described parentage confirmation must remain unconfirmed.
- Compound heterozygosity without demonstrated phase must remain phase unknown.
- Absence of a statement is missing information, not a negative observation.

## Retrieval and copyright

Preview metadata first. Retrieve PMC/openly licensed full text where available.
When full text is unavailable, clearly limit extraction to the abstract or
metadata. Quote only the minimum text needed and otherwise paraphrase with a
precise citation.
